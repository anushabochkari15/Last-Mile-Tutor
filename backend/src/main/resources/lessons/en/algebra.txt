What is it?
A linear equation has one unknown, usually x, and is solved by doing the same operation to both sides. The goal is to get x alone on one side.
Key rules
- Move constants across with the opposite sign.
- Divide both sides by the coefficient of x.
- Check your answer by substituting it back in.
Worked examples
Example 1: Solve 3x - 5 = 10
1. Add 5 to both sides: 3x = 15
2. Divide both sides by 3: x = 5
3. Check: 3(5) - 5 = 10
Answer: x = 5
Example 2: Solve 2x + 3 = 11
1. Subtract 3 from both sides: 2x = 8
2. Divide both sides by 2: x = 4
3. Check: 2(4) + 3 = 11
Answer: x = 4
Common mistake
Forgetting to change the sign when moving a term, or dividing only one side. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.
