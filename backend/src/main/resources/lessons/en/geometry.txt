What is it?
Geometry studies shapes, lengths, areas and angles. It lets us measure flat shapes and find unknown sides or angles using a few reliable rules.
Key rules
- Rectangle area equals length x width.
- Angles in any triangle add up to 180 degrees.
- In a right-angled triangle, a^2 + b^2 = c^2, where c is the hypotenuse.
Worked examples
Example 1: Find the area of a rectangle 6 cm by 4 cm
1. Write the formula: area = length x width
2. Substitute the values: 6 x 4
3. Multiply and add units: 24 cm^2
Answer: 24 cm^2
Example 2: A right triangle has sides 3 and 4. Find the hypotenuse.
1. Square the known sides: 3^2 = 9 and 4^2 = 16
2. Add them: 9 + 16 = 25
3. Take the square root: c = 5
Answer: hypotenuse = 5
Common mistake
Using the hypotenuse as one of the shorter sides, or mixing up units such as cm and m. The hypotenuse is always the longest side, opposite the right angle.
