What is it?
Probability measures how likely an event is. It equals favourable outcomes divided by total equally likely outcomes, and it always lies between 0 and 1.
Key rules
- 0 means impossible and 1 means certain.
- P(not A) = 1 - P(A).
- For independent events, multiply their probabilities.
Worked examples
Example 1: A fair die is rolled. Find P(even).
1. Favourable outcomes are 2, 4 and 6, so there are 3
2. Total outcomes are 1 to 6, so there are 6
3. Divide: 3/6 = 1/2
Answer: 1/2
Example 2: Two fair coins are tossed. Find P(both heads).
1. P(heads on the first coin) = 1/2
2. P(heads on the second coin) = 1/2
3. Multiply, since the events are independent: 1/2 x 1/2 = 1/4
Answer: 1/4
Common mistake
Counting outcomes that are not equally likely, or forgetting to divide by the total number of outcomes. Always list every outcome before counting favourable ones.
